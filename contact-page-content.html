The form on this page is how you reach me. I built SudokuSudoku and I run it, so a note comes to me. The page does not print an email address, and there is no phone number.

What to send

Bug reports are welcome. A button that does nothing, a page that fails to load, a puzzle that will not resume on this device, a daily that will not start, or a layout that covers the board all count. Say what you clicked, what you expected, and whether you were on a phone or a desktop.

Puzzle issues are welcome too. Send one when a hint names a move you cannot find on the board, when the game rejects a grid that follows the rules, or when a level does not match the hardest step you had to play. The gates are on how difficulty works. If you think a puzzle missed its window, name the level and what the hint called the move.

Technique questions are welcome. Ask when a page in the technique library looks wrong, when an elimination does not make sense, or when you can see a pattern and want its name. Row 1 is the top, column 1 is the left. The articles on how to play, common mistakes, and New York Times Medium and Hard are fair game if a sentence there does not match the board.

You can also ask to see, correct, or delete personal information: the email on an optional account, and whatever you put in this form. Delete my account, in Stats or Settings, removes the saved server copy yourself. Ideas are fine when they name a screen and what happened. Skip spam, threats, anything illegal, and someone else's personal information. Do not try to break the site, overload it, or take access you were not given. I can ignore a message. The terms of use are the rest of the rules.

What happens after you send it

I read what comes in. A reply, when there is one, usually takes days rather than hours. That is a habit, not a promise. Some notes do not need an answer. A same-day reply is not something to plan on. If a reply is useful, it goes to the email address you typed.

What you type is used to read the message and, if needed, to write back. A hidden field catches spam bots, and a filled-in one means the message is discarded. The visible check is Cloudflare Turnstile, which sends a token. Cloudflare may receive connection details such as your IP address. I do not publish your email address. If mail delivery is not configured, the form says it is not available yet and sends nothing. The rest is in the privacy policy.

A few things people ask

Where is my puzzle saved?

On this device, in the browser's local storage, so the same browser can resume it. An account is optional. In Stats or Settings, Save your progress emails you a sign-in link and a 6-digit code. There is no password. The code and the link expire after 15 minutes, and each works once. If you sign in, a copy of your stats, streak, settings, skill progress, and an unfinished puzzle is kept, so another device can pick it up. You can play without signing in. Delete my account removes that saved copy from the server. The copy on this device stays until you clear the site's data.

Can I print a puzzle?

No. The puzzles are for playing on the site.

Where do the puzzles come from?

They are generated in your browser when you start a game. The daily puzzle is the same Not Easy grid for everyone on a given UTC date, because it is seeded from that date. If a fresh grid will not land in the level you asked for, the game can serve a checked puzzle of that level that ships with the page. An unfinished puzzle stays on this device unless you sign in.

Is there an app?

There is no separate app in an app store. On a phone you can add the site to your home screen from the browser. The game, including a new puzzle and the daily, works offline after it has loaded once.

Where are the techniques?

The technique library walks through each step the solver uses. How difficulty works explains how those steps become a level. The articles cover how to play, common mistakes, and a measurement of New York Times Medium and Hard.

## About page version

Hi, I'm Ethan Ede.

I build websites for a living, and have for over 20 years. I also play a LOT of Sudoku. Most days it's the New York Times puzzle in their app, usually with coffee, sometimes with Teddy's head on my knee wondering why I'm not throwing the ball.

Here's the thing though. I'm not a Sudoku genius. I have a ceiling. For years I solved on singles and stubbornness, and when a puzzle needed an X-wing or a coloring chain, I'd guess or give up. I knew those techniques existed. I was too lazy to sit down and learn them, and every place I looked made it feel like homework: a wall of text, a static diagram, and good luck. So I built the tool I wanted. When you're stuck, it shows you the move on your own board, names it, explains why it works, and lets you practice that one technique until it clicks. The honest difficulty grades came along for the ride.

Most Sudoku sites grade puzzles by how many numbers are on the board. That's not really how it works. What makes a puzzle hard is the one move you can't see. So SudokuSudoku solves every puzzle one step at a time, the way a person would, and the hardest step sets the level. When you're stuck, the hint doesn't just fill in a number. It points at the move, names it, and links to a page that explains it with an example you can play with.

Is it perfect? Probably not. But it's honest, and it's built by someone who gets stuck too.

I live in Portland, Oregon, with my family, Teddy the Aussie labradoodle, and Mia the tabby. If something's broken, or you've got an idea, or you just want to argue about whether X-wings are fun, use the [contact form](/contact/). It comes straight to me.

-Ethan
